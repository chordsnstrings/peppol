# What is left

A survey of what is genuinely incomplete, run across eight independent lenses
over the whole codebase — unfinished threads the commit messages themselves
admit to, IFRS and IAS coverage, UAE regulatory coverage, wiring gaps, the
specific bug classes this codebase has produced more than once, test-coverage
holes, unfinished screens, and deployment risk.

Every candidate below was checked twice before it was allowed to stand: once
by a reader trying to refute it against the code, and once by a reader judging
whether a bookkeeper closing a month would actually hit it. Both had to let it
through. Seventy-one candidates were raised; fifty survived and twenty-one were
refuted. The refutations are kept at the end, because a gap that has already
been investigated and found not to exist is worth exactly as much as one that
does — it stops somebody spending a day on it twice.

Two things about how to read this. Effort estimates are for somebody who knows
this codebase. And the last section, "What is not left", is load-bearing: the
deliberate non-goals in the capability register are decisions, and closing them
would make the product worse, not better.

## Status

This survey is a snapshot of what was true when it ran. Most of it has since
been built, and the paragraphs below are kept exactly as written rather than
edited, because a description of a defect is the clearest statement of why the
fix is shaped the way it is — and because a survey rewritten to agree with the
code is a survey nobody can check.

**Closed since.** Every item under "Wrong numbers" — the PPE note's fictitious
additions, account 3300 missing from the SOCIE, the four-code cash list, money
hand-formatted at two decimals in fifteen modules, the two disagreeing hold
stores (including the create verb, which was found afterwards), the two
registers totalling a truncated page, and the missing 1060 in the monetary map.
Under "Regulatory exposure": the receivable write-off and Article 64(1) relief,
the FTA-assigned tax period and its stagger, GPSSA pension and the gratuity it
displaces, the margin-scheme purchase price, box 1's seven emirate rows, and
the Article 10 voluntary-disclosure population. Every item under "Unreachable"
— attachments, payroll amendment and settlement, the approvals engine on all
six posting paths, the seven unenforced permissions and forty-nine unguarded
routes, dimensions, delivery notes, contract modification, price closing and
list ending, marking time invoiced, unmatching a bank line, ending a
subscription, the credit checks at order acceptance, the notes pack's two
missing notes, the notification centre's four missing sources, multi-invoice
receipts, assessed-and-not-related, and trade finance's status filter and
cancellation. Under "Polish": workspace deletion, `clientIp`, the unbounded
export, the NUL byte, all twenty native dialogs, `closedBy` on a locked period,
the two `serialize` and `verify-ledger` items. And the separate, larger piece
the first section named — other comprehensive income, and a total that includes
it.

**Still open, deliberately.** IAS 38 intangibles as a class of their own: the
chart carries account 1560 and the cash flow statement classifies it, so the
balance sheet is right, but there is no IAS 38.118 reconciliation, no
indefinite-life path, and a capitalised licence still draws the IAS 16 policy
note. The arithmetic is right and the caption is wrong, at a year end only.

**Found while fixing, and fixed.** A drawing under a trade facility dated after
the facility expired. A related-party note reading only the first five thousand
documents. `outstandingOnOrder` scoped to the organisation and not the entity.
An approval threshold compared against a figure in another currency. A payment
run bypassing the approval rules entirely. A rule naming a ROLE satisfied by
any two people, because nothing checked what the signatories were.

What's left, ranked by whether it makes a number wrong, exposes you to a regulator, is simply unreachable from the product, or is polish. Everything below survived two rounds of refutation against the code; twenty other candidates did not, and I've listed those at the end so nobody re-opens them.

## Wrong numbers

These produce a figure that is incorrect on a statement, a note or a report, silently.

**The PPE note turns any revaluation or impairment into a fictitious addition and disposal.** `ppeNote` classifies 1500/1600/1590 movements purely by the sign of the line, with no source filter, while `revalueAsset` posts the IAS 16.35(b) elimination as Dr 1590 / Cr 1500 — so the note reads the whole accumulated depreciation as a disposal of cost and depreciation released on disposal, and the uplift as an addition. The same function is the IAS 36 impairment path, which is far commoner than the revaluation model, so an ordinary write-down triggers it too. `cost.agrees` cannot catch it because both sides come from the same lines. There is no line for revaluation increases/decreases (IAS 16.73(e)(iv)) or impairment losses and reversals (v)–(vi), and the accounting-policy note unconditionally states the cost model even for an entity that has revalued. `src/lib/server/ledger/equity.ts:1005-1022` and `:894-899`, `src/lib/server/ledger/asset-revaluation.ts:345-371`. The sibling `leaseNote` already filters by `source`, so the pattern to copy is one function away. **Medium effort** — partition by source, add the two movement rows fed from the AssetRevaluation events (the equity/profit split is already stored), branch the policy note on a 3300 balance, and add a test that revalues a depreciated asset and reads the note.

**Account 3300 is not one of the SOCIE's four hard-coded equity columns.** `EQUITY_COLUMNS` is a module constant — 3000, 3100, 3200, 3900 — with no configuration path, but 3300 ships in the standard chart and is credited by the revaluation module the product also ships. The movement falls into `unclassified`, the statement emits "Add it to the equity columns" (a remedy no user can perform) and then "This statement does not reconcile", with a difference exactly equal to the surplus. It re-fires every subsequent year even when 3300 doesn't move. Separately, `releaseSurplus` has its 3900 leg classified as `transfer_within_equity` while its 3300 leg is dropped, so that row nets to a credit instead of nil and nothing flags it. `src/lib/server/ledger/equity.ts:72, :666, :744-748, :787-793`, `src/lib/server/ledger/asset-revaluation.ts:452-490`. **Small effort** — add the account to the array, add a `revaluation` movement row, add a fixture asserting `reconciles === true`. There is also no OCI section in `profitAndLoss` at all, so no total comprehensive income figure exists anywhere; that is a separate, larger piece.

**Cash and cash equivalents is a four-code list that nothing derives from the chart.** `CASH_CODES = ["1000","1010","1020","1050"]` is declared three times and imported by two more modules; the petty-cash screen's "Cash account" field is free text and the cheque and AR/AP receipt paths accept any code, so money genuinely lands outside the four. In a period where such an account *moves*, the cash flow statement declares itself unreconciled and tells the user to edit a list no screen exposes — loud but unactionable. In a period where it holds a balance and does not move, no warning fires at all: opening and closing cash on a primary IAS 7 statement are silently understated by the whole balance, and the same wrong definition flows into the direct method and the comparatives cash trend. `src/lib/server/ledger/cashflow.ts:61, :325-326, :351-352, :487`, `equity.ts:75`, `forecast.ts:377`, `cashflow-direct.ts:46`, `comparatives.ts:1005`. **Medium effort** — one helper resolving cash from accounts with subtype BANK or CASH, falling back to the four seeded codes; plus a subtype control on the chart screen, since no screen can set it today.

**Fifteen ledger modules hand-format money at two decimals regardless of currency.** `fmtMinor` knows each currency's exponent and eight modules use it; fifteen carry a private `padStart(3,"0")`/`slice(-2)` helper. `kpi.ts` is handed the real book currency and ignores it; `corptax.ts` and `deferred-tax.ts` hard-code the string "AED". This is confined to error and narrative text — no posted amount is wrong — but `post.ts:314` formats the *transaction* currency, and multi-currency posting into an AED book is first-class here, so a UAE business with a Kuwaiti or Bahraini supplier is told an entry is "out by 0.01" for a one-fils imbalance, in a sentence naming KWD two words earlier. `src/lib/server/ledger/post.ts:436`, plus `asset-revaluation.ts:67`, `close.ts:191`, `corptax.ts:827`, `deferred-tax.ts:1332`, `kpi.ts:105`, `landed-cost.ts:74`, `leave.ts:90`, `opening.ts:74`, `payroll.ts:79`, `procurement.ts:112`, `provisions.ts:361`, `recurring.ts:82`, `revenue.ts:643`, `timesheets.ts:513`. `month-end.ts:74-85` is the one that was already fixed and its comment names the class. **Medium effort**, entirely mechanical — plus a rule in `verify-swedish-design.mjs`, which currently walks only `src/app/(app)/accounting` and matches `/100` division rather than string surgery.

**Two hold stores that disagree, and the permissive one is the credit controller's screen.** `placeOnHold` (Customers screen) writes only `Counterparty.onHold`; `credit-control.ts` derives holds exclusively from `CreditHold` rows and never reads the flag. So a hold placed from Customers produces `decision: "allow"` from `creditCheck`, counts as 0 in the register's on-hold summary, and shows empty hold history — while the Customers chip and `checkCreditBeforeSale` both honour it. The screen states the opposite at the moment of the act: "A hold stops the next order." Credit *limits* were unified (`setCreditLimit` mirrors, `limitFrom` falls back), which makes holds the inconsistent case rather than a design stance. `src/lib/server/ledger/counterparties.ts:1089-1147`, `src/lib/server/ledger/credit-control.ts:465, :549, :775, :845, :949, :1804`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/customers/page.tsx:241`. **Small effort** — delegate `placeOnHold`/`releaseHold` to the structured pair, plus a backfill for already-flagged counterparties.

**`deliveryRegister` computes its four headline counts from a truncated 500-note page.** Take 500 ordered newest-first, then derive total/draft/dispatched/delivered by filtering that array. An entity past 500 notes in the window sees "Notes 500" and a breakdown of an arbitrary slice presented as the period totals, with no notice — against this codebase's own convention (`analytics.ts:148-151` and `projects.ts:217-224` both return `truncated` and refuse to total a partial set). The accounting-critical figure on the same screen, `deliveredNotInvoiced`, is uncapped and correct. `src/lib/server/ledger/deliveries.ts:505, :536-545`. **Small effort** — a `groupBy` for the counts, `take: 501` plus a flag.

**`priceListRegister` applies the list filter in memory after `take: 2000`.** Past 2,000 rows across all lists, clicking "prices" on a list shows only the fraction inside the first 2,000 by item code, the per-list counts understate for the same reason, and the findings block emits "X is in force and prices nothing" — a flatly false statement — for any list whose codes sort past the cut. `resolvePrice` queries per item with no cap, so quotes and documents are unaffected. `src/lib/server/ledger/pricing.ts:561-565, :584-585, :588-589, :625-629`. **Small effort** — push `priceListId` into the where, count with `groupBy`.

**`revaluation.ts` lists 2060 in its monetary code map but not its 1060 mirror.** A foreign-currency cheque the entity *writes* gets an unrealised FX difference; the identical cheque it *holds* is skipped as "carries no subtype", so the P&L is understated by that amount. The skip is shown on screen with its reason, and the remedy the message prescribes — give it a subtype — cannot be done from any screen, since the chart editor has no subtype control. AED's USD peg means only EUR/GBP/INR post-dated cheques bite. `src/lib/server/ledger/revaluation.ts:124, :134`. **One token** to add `"1060"`, plus a test holding the map against `UAE_CHART` the way `test/ledger-cashflow.test.ts:270-285` does for `CLASSIFICATION`. Note the 1255/2470 half of the original report was refuted: trade-finance never passes a currency on any journal line, so those never reach the classifier — which is its own separate bug (a facility records a currency it then ignores).

## Regulatory exposure

Nothing here is arithmetic; it is what you can and cannot file, and what the FTA would find.

**No receivable write-off exists, and 1100 refuses a manual journal, so Article 64(1) bad-debt relief is unreachable.** `ar.ts` exports exactly three operations and none of them derecognises a debt. Writing one off must credit 1100, which is `isControl`, and `post.ts:281` refuses control accounts on `source: "manual"` while the journals route hard-codes that source. So an uncollectible invoice cannot leave the sales ledger by any route — it ages in the over-90 bucket forever, inflates credit exposure and every dunning list, and the 5% output VAT already paid to the FTA on money never received cannot be recovered. The product describes the relief in detail (`credit-control.ts:1334-1341`), warns the customer about it in the dunning letter, and then provides no way to take it. The only workaround, a tax credit note, understates turnover and reduces box 1 taxable supplies instead of recording an adjustment. Separately, account 1150 is seeded and read by five modules and written by none, so the receivables note can only ever assert "No allowance for doubtful debts has been recognised" — a positive statement an auditor will challenge. `src/lib/server/ledger/ar.ts:85, :258, :337`, `setup.ts:44`, `vat.ts:102-118`, `equity.ts:929-938, :1209`. **Large effort.** The cheapest valuable half is the write-off plus a recorded notification date and a bad-debt `sourceType` that `vat.ts` recognises so box 1 can carry an Adjustment column — that half returns cash.

**No box 6 or box 7: import VAT on goods has no tax code.** The return defines four sales boxes and two expense boxes. There is no import tax code in `types.ts`; reverse charge is implemented for services only; `vat-schemes.ts` correctly identifies goods leaving a designated zone as an import and then hands back `STANDARD_5`. Box 6 is FTA-pre-populated from customs against the importer's TRN and is not editable except through box 7 — so coding an import as reverse charge double-counts against the pre-populated figure, and coding it as an ordinary expense leaves box 6 with no matching recovery. The bookkeeper cannot patch either, because 2100 and 1350 refuse manual journals. Worst of it: the return's own self-reconciliation against 2100/1350 reports `outputMatches: true` on a return missing an entire output box, so the honesty mechanism confirms an unfilable return. `src/lib/server/ledger/vat.ts:85-99`, `src/lib/domain/types.ts:35-41`, `ap.ts:35, :179, :185`, `vat-schemes.ts:1259-1285`, `faf.ts:70, :444`. **Medium effort** — an import profile posting a distinctly tagged output/input pair via the `sourceType` mechanism `vat-schemes.ts` already uses, box 6 with recovery in box 9, a box 7 channel, and a customs declaration number so the FAF permit column stops being empty. Import of goods is the normal case for a UAE trading business, so this is the highest-value item in this group.

**Nothing stores the FTA-assigned tax period.** `lastCompletedQuarter` derives the period from `Math.floor(month/3)`, three times over (`attention.ts`, `notifications.ts`, `forecast.ts`), and the VAT screen's picker offers only calendar quarters and calendar months. There is no field on any entity-level model recording frequency or stagger. A taxpayer on a Feb/May/Aug/Nov stagger gets, every quarter, a confident "AED X payable to the FTA" for the wrong three months against a due date a month late — from the module whose own header says it exists to stop somebody missing a VAT deadline. A monthly filer gets one reminder per three returns. The attention screen names its two other inferences in a footnote and is silent on this one. `src/lib/server/ledger/attention.ts:127, :159-167, :339-395`, `notifications.ts:249-263`, `forecast.ts:360-369`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/vat/page.tsx:19-33`. **Medium effort** — one entity setting, one shared `taxPeriodFor()` replacing the three copies, and generate the picker from it.

**No partial-exemption apportionment of input tax.** Exempt supplies are reported in box 5 and box 9 recovers input tax in full, with no ratio, no annual wash-up and no warning. Residential rent, bare land, local passenger transport and margin-based financial services are all exempt, so every residential landlord, mixed-use property company and finance business is partly exempt and is handed an over-recovering return. Two things make it worse than a missing feature: the correction is structurally blocked (1350 is a control account, so no manual write-back is possible, and coding the purchase to a non-standard profile drops the net expense out of box 9 too), and the codebase warns loudly about the far rarer margin scheme using data it has in the same pass, while saying nothing here. `src/lib/server/ledger/vat.ts:93, :268, :295-329, :363`, `expenses.ts:21-29`. **Large effort.** The cheap first move is a warning when box 5 is non-nil and input tax is claimed — that alone removes the silence.

**A correction re-dated out of a filed quarter is never measured against the AED 10,000 threshold.** `reverse()` refuses a closed period, so a correction to a filed quarter necessarily lands in an open one and flows into the current return as ordinary movement. Nothing measures its size, compares it to the Article 10 threshold, or starts the 20-business-day clock. The product already takes a position on which quarters are filed — `attention.ts:355-375` reads a locked quarter as filed and runs a 28-day statutory clock off exactly that proxy — so the "we can't know what was filed" defence doesn't apply. `src/lib/server/ledger/post.ts:356-424`, `vat.ts:203`, `attention.ts:55-61`. **Medium effort** — select reversals whose original falls in an earlier filed-proxy period, sum the 2100/1350 movement, warn above the threshold naming the entries and the test. Report the population, never a verdict; the ledger cannot distinguish an error from a legitimate Article 61/62 credit note.

**Payroll accrues Article 51 gratuity for every employee and has no GPSSA pension.** `Employee` has no nationality field and `gratuityAccrual` runs unconditionally. For a UAE or GCC national that books a liability that is not owed to 2250/6050, omits the employer pension contribution entirely, and never deducts the employee share — so net pay, the SIF file and the bank transfer all overpay every month. There is no workaround: `deductionAccount` is a whole-run override defaulting to Employee advances, and correcting 2250 by hand permanently breaks `payrollSummary`'s reconciliation. The payroll screen states flatly that end-of-service is provided under Article 51, the article that limits the entitlement to "the full-time foreign worker." `src/lib/server/ledger/payroll.ts:41, :613-673, :813-822, :1370-1382`, `prisma/schema.prisma:731-764`, `setup.ts:69-95, :122-138`. **Large effort.** The UAE private-sector workforce is overwhelmingly expatriate so many customers never hit it, but Emiratisation quotas point at exactly the 20-plus-employee firms this targets.

**Margin-scheme invoices have no purchase-cost field.** The tax profile dropdown renders `TAX_PROFILE_LIST` whole, MARGIN_SCHEME included, and the only conditional field the line editor renders is `exemptionReason`. `marginPurchaseMinor` is set by no screen or route body anywhere. Both the invoice composer and the VAT return warn about it — loudly and accurately — but both point at a remedy the product refuses: there is no field to enter the cost, and 2100 rejects a manual journal for the resulting tax. So a used-goods dealer is told to add a figure the screen doesn't have and post an amount the ledger won't accept. `src/lib/domain/types.ts:95`, `tax.ts:121, :142, :174-187`, `advice.ts:61`, `src/app/(app)/invoices/new/page.tsx:589-602`, `vat.ts:321-329`. **Small effort** — one money input beside the existing conditional. When you add it, condition the VAT-return warning on the tax actually being absent, or it will cry wolf on correctly-handled periods.

**VAT 201 box 1 is one flat row, not seven emirate rows.** `JournalLine.taxEmirate` exists with a comment explaining exactly why, is set on every AR revenue and output-tax line, persisted and exported to CSV — and read back by nothing. `vat.ts` groups on `taxCode` alone. For a single-establishment business this means copying one figure to one row, which is trivial; for a multi-emirate one it is a real second pass. Note the capture is not yet trustworthy either: `ar.ts:190` takes the seller's *registered* emirate, so the column is constant per entity and would produce a confidently wrong seven-row split if read as-is — and `exports.ts:369` already ships that uniform column to CSV where it reads as authoritative. `src/lib/server/ledger/vat.ts:85-94, :232`, `ar.ts:190`, `prisma/schema.prisma:484-486`. **Medium effort**, most of which is deciding where the emirate of supply comes from rather than the grouping.

## Unreachable — built, tested, routed, and not on any screen

This is the largest group by count and the cheapest to close. In every case the server side is complete and it is the client that is missing.

**Attachments have a module, an API, a schema and tests, and no upload, list, view or download anywhere.** Nothing in `src/app/(app)` or `src/components` imports the module or fetches the route; the only trace is a count chip on the audit screen that is a dash on every row for every tenant, forever. The SHA-256 re-hash tamper check — the single reason the module exists — can never fire. `src/lib/server/ledger/attachments.ts`, `src/app/api/ledger/attachments/route.ts` (all three verbs implemented), `src/app/(app)/accounting/audit/page.tsx:283-286`. `docs/design/component-inventory.md:595` already specifies the drill chain that ends at an attachment. **Medium effort** — one client component mounted on bill, claim, journal, asset and bank-line screens.

**Payroll cannot amend an employee or settle a leaver.** `updateEmployee` and `settleEndOfService` are routed at `route.ts:77` and `:105`; the screen sends only add-employee, run, post, pay and wps. `payrollRun` skips a person only when `leftOn < start`, and `settleEndOfService` is the only writer of `leftOn`, so a leaver keeps drawing a full salary, a WPS row, a gratuity increment and a leave provision every month — and `payrollSummary` counts them on both sides, so its reconciliation cannot detect it. Article 53 requires settlement within fourteen days. A pay rise is equally impossible, forcing a second employee code that restarts the gratuity clock. `src/app/api/ledger/payroll/route.ts:77, :105`, `src/lib/server/ledger/payroll.ts:371, :624, :983, :1326`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/payroll/page.tsx:123, :333`. **Medium effort**, client-only — two per-row forms; the module already refuses a joining-date change once payslips are posted.

**The approvals module is inert.** `assertApproved` is exported, tested, and imported by nothing; its own doc block names the four call sites and none of them import it. And `pendingFor` seeds only from expense claims plus subjects that already carry a decision row, while the only surface that can decide is the queue itself — so a bill can only reach the queue if it is already in the queue. Meanwhile the screen says "No approval rules yet, so nothing in this entity needs a signature. Write one and it applies from the next document raised", and defaults the editor to BILL. Cash leaving the bank is still independently controlled (`PaymentRun.approvedBy` plus its CHECK; expenses refuse self-approval), so this is a false assurance about thresholded multi-signature policy rather than an unguarded path to money. `src/lib/server/ledger/approvals.ts:700-717, :772-775, :783`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/approvals/page.tsx:157, :197, :277`. **Large effort** — four call sites plus a submission write so the other four subject types can enter a queue.

**Seven permissions are enforced nowhere and two more only partly.** Of 21 keys, 14 reach a `requirePermission`; `ledger.read`, `ap.manage`, `payroll.run`, `payroll.read`, `expense.approve`, `tax.file` and `bank.reconcile` appear only in the catalogue and its test. Only 12 of 69 ledger route files call it at all — `payroll`, `expenses`, `vat`, `corptax`, `bank`, `bank-import`, `assets`, `ap/post`, `ar/post` all lack it. The shipped VIEWER role's own description says "Payroll is not included: salaries are not a general read", and a Viewer can POST `/api/ledger/payroll` with action run. The roles screen says "Grant one role and enforcement begins", and `createRole` refuses unknown keys on the stated grounds that "a permission nobody can check is a permission nobody holds" — which is what seven shipped keys are. `src/lib/server/ledger/permissions.ts:52, :145`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/roles/page.tsx:87-93`. **Large effort** by count, trivial per route — plus a check in `verify-ledger-api.mjs` enumerating `PERMISSIONS` and failing on a key that appears in no route file.

**Dimensions have no write UI anywhere, and no posting can be tagged.** The three write actions appear in no screen; `entry-grid.tsx`, the only manual journal form, has no dimension column despite `post()` accepting one per line; `requiresDimension` appears in the chart screen only inside an unrendered interface. So Cost centres and the IFRS 8 segment note are permanently all-Unallocated for any browser user. The segments screen tells the user to "Create one on the cost centres screen and tag postings with it" — that screen has neither control. `src/app/api/ledger/dimensions/route.ts:82, :90, :94`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/dimensions/page.tsx:107`, `src/components/ledger/entry-grid.tsx`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/segments/page.tsx:165, :521`. **Medium effort.** `projects.ts:417` does create a PROJECT dimension from the projects screen, but with no way to tag postings its columns are still empty.

**No screen can raise a delivery note.** The register has dispatch/confirm/cancel/return per row and no create control; `createDeliveryNote` is called only by tests. So the empty state is a dead end, the "Delivered, not invoiced" tab renders zero for every entity forever, and the four row actions can never have a row. `src/app/(app)/accounting/deliveries/page.tsx`, `src/lib/server/ledger/deliveries.ts:144`, `src/app/api/ledger/deliveries/route.ts:66`. **Medium effort** — a create panel; the peer screens (sales-orders, procurement, landed-cost, subscriptions) all follow the opposite pattern, so this is the exception.

**A contract's price cannot be changed or a contract cancelled from the revenue screen.** `modifyContract` and `cancelContract` are routed and tested; the screen sends six other actions. A variation order is the ordinary event in a construction or services contract, and `recordBilling` hard-refuses billing past the original price with the message "If the price has changed, modify the contract first" — an instruction the UI cannot obey. Recognition corrects to a target derived from the stale price, so revenue and the 1310/2310 presentation stay pinned to it; a manual journal doesn't help because `postedFor` filters on `sourceType`, so the run doesn't see it and the register's reconciliation goes false permanently. `src/app/api/ledger/revenue/route.ts:61, :87`, `src/lib/server/ledger/revenue.ts:201, :330`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/revenue/page.tsx`. **Small effort** — two controls in the existing obligations panel.

**A price cannot be closed, so a price rise is impossible from the UI.** The AddPrice form always posts an open-ended `validFrom`, so the next price for the same item and break trips the `PriceListEntry_no_overlap` gist exclusion, and `setPrices` returns "Close the old price to the day before the new one starts" — an instruction with no control behind it. The same trap repeats one level up on default lists. The register already returns each row's id, which is exactly the `entryId` `closePrice` needs; the screen uses it only as a React key. `priceVariance` — "the report the list exists for" — is likewise unreachable, though it takes `chargedMinor` as an input so it is a typed comparison rather than an automatic sweep. `src/lib/server/ledger/pricing.ts:207-217, :224, :499, :594`, `src/app/(app)/accounting/pricing/page.tsx:475-568`. **Small effort.**

**Timesheets cannot mark time as invoiced, so 1330 only leaves by write-off.** `markInvoiced` and the route's `case "invoice"` are never called, and nothing else in the product sets a time entry to invoiced. `wipAt` carries draft and approved entries, so write-off — recorded as time that could not be billed — is the only exit. Worse than a growing asset: every WIP run credits 5100 with the cost held back, so with no invoicing path the cost of all billable time ever recorded stays permanently out of the income statement. A manual Dr 5100 / Cr 1330 is self-reversing, because the next run measures against what 1330 actually holds. The screen renders an "invoiced" filter and an invoice-id column for a state it cannot reach, and `recoveryBps` is structurally 0% for any screen-only user. `src/lib/server/ledger/timesheets.ts:203, :261-272`, `src/app/api/ledger/timesheets/route.ts:69`. **Small effort** — one bulk button mirroring the write-off handler.

**The bank screen never lists matched lines, so a wrong match cannot be undone.** `unmatch` exists, is tested with the comment "so a mistake can be undone", and is routed; nothing calls it, and `reconcile()` returns only the two *unmatched* lists, so a matched line vanishes from the page entirely. The arithmetic stays right (equal amounts are forced), so the damage is the itemisation — the outstanding-cheque working paper names the wrong cheque and cannot be corrected. The sharper everyday case is `post`: it posts and auto-matches, so a slip in the five-item contra dropdown is locked in, and reversing leaves the bank line matched to the reversed line with the reversal's own line as a phantom outstanding item forever. `src/lib/server/ledger/bank.ts:280, :398-434`, `src/app/api/ledger/bank/route.ts:74`. **Small effort** — return matched pairs, render a third panel, wire the button.

**A subscription cannot be ended, and a quote or order cannot be edited.** Ending: only a mid-term cancellation is stranded (a subscription with an `endsOn` self-ends), and ARR already counts only active, so no figure is wrong. ## Polish, and one operational item

**Workspace delete removes 10 of 94 org-scoped tables.** `prisma.organization.delete()` succeeds because `Membership` is the only model with an `Organization` relation in the entire schema — every ledger model carries a bare `orgId String`. So journals, bank lines, base64 receipt scans, employee salaries and named key-management compensation stay in production forever, unreachable by any code path because nothing can enumerate an org that no longer exists. That is the PDPL erasure path, and the route's own docstring says "and all its data". A naive cascade fix will not work: `gl_entry_guard_trg` is a BEFORE DELETE trigger that refuses to delete a posted entry, so erasure has to be a deliberate path that suspends it. The reset half of the original report was wrong — entity ids are freshly minted on re-onboarding, so the old ledger is orphaned and invisible rather than additive, and the first trial balance is correct. `src/app/api/admin/tenants/[org]/delete/route.ts:28-40`, `prisma/schema.prisma:82`, `prisma/migrations/20260903041350_ledger_core/migration.sql:358-401`. **Medium effort** — one purge module driven off the Prisma DMMF so model 104 cannot be silently forgotten, plus a test asserting the DMMF list minus the purge list is empty.

## What is not left

Do not spend time on these. Several were investigated this session and are decisions, not gaps.

The register's "What this does not do" list is authoritative and correct: it does not file anything; it does not detect which parties are related; it does not do tax depreciation, group stock, migration history or Benford; a trial-balance migration brings balances not history, and the import screen says so in a red note above the paste box; a price list never converts a currency; the capital-asset scheme takes taxable use as an input. `postInvoice` deliberately does not consult credit control, and the docstring explains why. Control accounts refusing `source: "manual"` is the design. Consolidation applies no currency translation, and it says so on screen in a panel headed "Read these before using the figures", with a regression test asserting the warning fires — the only thing missing there is a line in the register's non-goal list. Leases are lessee-only, stated in the IFRS 16 note's own `notDerivable` list, which is rendered on the notes screen and held by a test. Corporate tax excludes transfer pricing under Articles 34-36, stated in the module header and on the screen.

And these were reported and refuted against the code, so they should not come back: comparatives *are* restated automatically because the prior column is a live query rather than a stored snapshot, and prior-period correction *is* possible via the distinct `period.reopen` permission; the AccountBalance cache *is* reconciled against journal lines by `dimensions.ts`, per account and by magnitude, asserted about fifteen times in its tests; `suggestMatches` filtering to `posted` is correct because it matches identities, not balances, and the reversing half has the opposite sign so it can never be offered; the price-list EXCLUDE constraints *are* attacked at the database, via SQLSTATE 23P01 rather than by constraint name; the dunning worst-first work list *does* have a screen, on `/accounting/customers` under "Who to chase, worst first"; delivery "return all" *is* idempotent, keyed on `RTN/{number}/{lineNo}` through `priorMovement`; subscription invoices cannot post with lines that don't foot, because `ar.ts:135-143` refuses; stock location and batch orphans are *caught* by the inventory tie and reported per SKU; the route sweep's top-level enumeration doesn't matter because all six "missed" routes are POST-only and named individually in other suites; migrations, connection pooling and the SheetJS CDN dependency are all deployment concerns the repo deliberately does not own; and the three ledger indexes without `CONCURRENTLY` run in the same migration burst that creates the tables, so they can never meet a row.
